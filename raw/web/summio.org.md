# Summio

> Summio is a free iPhone app that turns books, YouTube videos, long articles, and PDFs into structured AI summaries you can chat with. Available in 27 languages on iOS 18 and later.

## What Summio is

Summio is an **AI reading companion for iPhone**. It generates editorial summaries of:

- **Books** — full-length non-fiction and fiction, searched by title or author across a 100,000+ catalogue
- **YouTube videos** — lectures, podcasts, talks, playlists of any duration
- **Long articles** — newsletters, magazine pieces, web essays
- **PDFs** — research papers, reports, manuals, scanned books (OCR is automatic)

Every summary is rendered as a chaptered editorial reading object with quotes, key ideas, glossary, takeaways, and source citations. You can choose one of four depth levels — short, medium, long, or detailed.

## Key features

- **Dialogue** — chat with the full text of any book, video, article, or PDF you've summarised. Answers cite the source passage.
- **Practice** — spaced-repetition flashcards and recall prompts generated from the material you saved.
- **Library** — searchable, offline, syncs across iPhone and iPad.
- **27 interface languages** — English, Russian, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Arabic, Turkish, Polish, Ukrainian, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Czech, Hungarian, Indonesian, Malay, Filipino, Kazakh, Uzbek.
- **Five tones of voice** — professional, friendly, succinct, story-driven, scholarly.
- **Source-grounded AI** — every claim in a summary points back to a passage in the source.

## Common comparisons

- **vs Blinkist** — Summio summarises *any* source you point it at (books, videos, articles, PDFs), not just a curated catalogue of titles. AI-native; the catalogue is the entire long-form web.

- **vs ChatGPT or Claude on a PDF** — Summio keeps a persistent searchable library, generates structured editions instead of one-shot responses, and ties chat to the exact summary and source.

## What is RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)? — https://summio.org/glossary/rag-retrieval-augmented-generation
RAG is an AI pattern where a model retrieves relevant passages from a source document before generating an answer, grounding output in the source.

A vanilla LLM answers from fixed training data and is prone to hallucination. RAG adds retrieval: search the corpus (book, PDF, knowledge base) for relevant passages, hand them to the LLM as context, then generate an answer citing specific passages.

RAG quality depends on chunking, embedding for similarity search, and prompting. Summio uses RAG to ground every summary and chat claim back to a passage in the original book, video transcript, article, or PDF — if it cannot cite a passage, it does not print the claim.

## What is AI book summary? — https://summio.org/glossary/ai-book-summary
An AI book summary is an automatically generated condensed version of a book, produced by a large language model from the book's text or transcript.

Good AI summaries keep structure (chapter order), voice (author's register), and citations (every claim points at a source passage). Summio generates source-grounded summaries at four depths in 27 languages with full-text chat afterwards.

## How to Summarise a Book with AI — https://summio.org/how-to/summarize-a-book-with-ai
Total time: 5 min. Requirements: iPhone, Summio app (free), book title or ISBN.

1. **Pick an AI book summary tool** — look for open input (any title), depth control (short/medium/long/detailed), and source-grounded citations. Summio satisfies all three; Blinkist/Headway are catalogue-locked; ChatGPT can summarise but does not cite passages.
2. **Add the book** — search by title/author (100,000+ catalogue), paste ISBN, or upload PDF for self-published works.
3. **Choose depth** — short for triage (5 min), medium for comprehension (15 min), long/detailed for study (30–60 min with chapters, quotes, glossary).
4. **Read then chat** — ask follow-up questions; every answer cites a passage in the original.
5. **Save and drill** — library storage plus spaced-repetition flashcards from key ideas, glossary terms, and pull quotes.

Tips: verify claims before citing in writing; use short depth as triage layer; summaries are not substitutes for full books.
